QUESTION 3a: FLOCKS
I found three flocks by plotting the beta angle for every satellite. At first some
satellites seemed to be missing from the plot, and I realized their lines were hiding
behind each other because their values are almost the same. Satellites launched
together share the same orbit, so their beta angles line up.
- Flock 1: alpha, charlie, foxtrot
- Flock 2: bravo, echo, golf
- Flock 3: delta. It starts close to flock 2, but its line pulls away over the year
  (from about 0.3 to about 2.0 degrees apart from echo), so it is in a different
  orbit and was likely launched separately.

QUESTION 3b: DANGEROUS ANOMALIES
1. Delta can't control its rotation. In delta's plot, wheel_y and wheel_z stay at 0
   the whole time, so only 2 of its 4 wheels are working. The other wheels hit
   saturation in clusters, and on June 2 gyro_z moves steadily past -2 deg/s toward
   -4 deg/s, which means delta is spinning and can't stop. Its gyro rates are above
   2 deg/s in either direction for 5.88% of points, more than 5 times any other
   satellite. A spinning satellite can't point its camera, solar panels, or antenna.

2. Bravo, echo, and golf have low voltage. In bravo's plot, the low voltage flag
   wasn't a flat line like alpha's. It was a series of spikes that got more frequent
   over time. Echo's flag started at 0, switched on and off, and then stayed on, and
   its data stops on June 1. Golf showed a milder version. Their bus voltage also
   stops recovering to about 8.3 V each orbit. The flag is active 35.71% (echo),
   20.51% (bravo), and 1.47% (golf) of the time, and each time it shuts off hardware
   like the camera. Since all three are in the same flock, I think the cause could be
   something they share. One possibility is their orbit: flock 2's beta angle is
   closer to 0 than flock 1's, which means longer time in Earth's shadow and less
   time to recharge.

QUESTION 4: ANOMALY FUNCTIONS
Each function takes one telemetry channel and returns True or False.
- is_stuck_at_zero: True if every reading is 0. It finds delta's dead wheels
  (wheel_y and wheel_z) and delta's bus voltage channel.
- has_low_voltage_events: True if the low voltage flag is ever on. It finds bravo,
  echo, and golf.
- is_spinning_out_of_control: True if a gyro rate stays above 2 deg/s for 30 minutes
  in a row. It finds delta's gyro_z.

I ran every function on all 7 satellites. Alpha, charlie, and foxtrot return False
for every check, so there are no false positives.

My first spin check averaged readings over 10 minutes, and it wrongly flagged echo
and golf. The gyro data seems to be recorded more often during turns, so short turns
outweighed the quiet periods. I fixed it by taking the median rate for each minute
first, so every minute counts the same.

ASSUMPTIONS AND DATA NOTES
- Seven of the csv files have column names on the first line. beta_sun_deg.csv was the
  only one with a different layout (one column per satellite), so I loaded it
  separately from the telemetry files.
- Sensors report at different times, so for each check I counted only the rows where
  that check's sensors have a reading.
- A wheel counts as saturated at 8000 rpm or more, and a gyro counts at more than
  2 deg/s, in either direction.
- Timestamps are Unix milliseconds, which I converted to UTC.
- Delta's bus voltage is flat at 0 the whole time, but its low voltage flag never
  turns on and its wheels and gyros keep working. So I think the voltage sensor is
  broken, not the power itself.
- Foxtrot has one bus voltage reading of about 2 V, but the low voltage flag didn't
  turn on and the readings before and after are normal, so it is likely a bad reading.
- Straight lines in some plots are gaps in the data, not real measurements.
- The beta file covers June 2018 to June 2019, and the telemetry covers May 30 to
  June 2, 2019. The files use "alpha" while the instructions say "alfa."
